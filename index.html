V2G / Bidirectional Charging for Heavy-Duty Truck Depots

Thesis idea map for TRATON / MAN R&D · "UnPlugged Energisers" · Efficient Transport  ·  19 June 2026

This is an opinionated map written so you can imagine and choose. Every idea has a flow diagram. The core message: build novelty on the three things only you can do — sit inside the OEM that writes the warranty, test a real truck + charger + V2G, and compare EU vs China. Don't make "a bigger Simulink model" the contribution; make a new decision function the contribution.

0 · The one thing that changes everything — your unfair advantages

Most V2G theses are written by people with none of your three assets. Build the novelty on these.

AssetWhy it is rareWhat it unlocks
You sit inside the OEM that writes the battery warranty Academics model the owner's fear ("will V2G void my warranty?"). Nobody optimizes from the OEM's side, where the warranty is a design variable. Warranty-budget-coupled dispatch + warranty product co-design (Idea 2).
Real truck + charger + a few days of real V2G Almost every degradation model in V2G optimization is calibrated on lab cells, then scaled. Nobody closes the loop on a real HD pack under commanded bidirectional events. Sim-to-real calibration / residual learning (Idea 1) — your credibility anchor.
EU + China is in your job description Most comparisons are descriptive tables. A reusable policy compiler (Idea 5) — an internal TRATON market-entry tool.
Hard truth on novelty (verified against current literature): "Add a battery-aging layer," "build a digital twin," "do MPC instead of MILP," "compare NMC vs LFP," and "chance-constrained FCR bidding" are all already published. A thesis that only does those is good engineering, weak research. The novelty must live in a new decision function; the digital twin is the validation environment, not the contribution.

2 · The ideas (ranked by novelty × feasibility × TRATON value)

★ Idea 1 — Sim-to-Real "Degradation Twin Calibration" (credibility anchor)

Novelty: HighFeasibility: Med–High Needs real truckRole: validation anchor

Gap: V2G economics hinge on degradation cost per kWh — yet that number is taken from lab cells and assumed to transfer to a 400+ kWh truck pack. Pack thermal gradients, BMS balancing, inverter losses and real C-rate tapers differ. No published measured-stress → degradation calibration exists for a real heavy-duty pack under commanded bidirectional events.

New function: run your few-day V2G test, extract measured stress signatures, compute the residual between the in-house lab aging model and real pack behaviour, and emit a truck-specific corrected degradation cost surface for the optimizer.

flowchart LR
    LAB["In-house lab aging model (cell-level)"] --> PRED["Predicted stress to fade"]
    subgraph TEST["Few-day real truck + charger V2G test"]
        T1["Commanded profiles (vary C-rate, DoD, SoC window)"]
        T2["Measure: RT efficiency, taper, response delay, dT, dV/dQ"]
    end
    T1 --> T2 --> MEAS["Measured stress signatures"]
    PRED --> RES{"Residual = measured - predicted"}
    MEAS --> RES
    RES --> CORR["Truck-specific corrected cost surface c(SoC,DoD,C,T)"]
    CORR --> OPT["Feeds Optimizer Layer L4"]
    RES --> INSIGHT["Insight: where lab models over/under-estimate pack life"]

Honest note: true capacity fade needs weeks/months — so measure stress proxies (efficiency, taper, delay, thermal, impedance snapshot), not fade itself, and validate the proxies match the model's inputs. Still novel.

★★ Idea 2 — Warranty-Budget-Coupled V2G Dispatch (the OEM-only idea)

Novelty: Very HighFeasibility: Medium TRATON value: CriticalRole: HEADLINE

Gap (verified): existing warranty+V2G work is owner-side ("performance guarantees," "will my capacity drop"). The OEM-side question is open: MAN writes the warranty (e.g. 80% SoH at min(N years, Y kWh throughput)). V2G spends that budget. How should the optimizer treat remaining warranty throughput as a depletable, shadow-priced resource — and which warranty product makes V2G net-positive for both MAN and the fleet? This couples controller design to contract design.

New function: add a warranty-state variable W(t); each V2G kWh draws it down. The dual on the W-constraint is the shadow price of warranty — the true marginal cost of cycling. Then run contract co-design over warranty product variants.

flowchart TB
    WARR["Warranty: SoH>=80% at min(N years, Y kWh)"] --> BUDGET["Warranty budget W(t) = remaining throughput"]
    BUDGET --> CONS["Constraint: sum of V2G throughput <= W(t)"]
    PRICES["Market revenue (arb / FCR / V2B)"] --> OBJ["Objective: max revenue - degradation - warranty drawdown"]
    CONS --> OBJ
    OBJ --> SOLVE["Solve MILP / MPC"]
    SOLVE --> DUAL["Dual on W = SHADOW PRICE OF WARRANTY (euro/kWh)"]
    DUAL --> DECIDE{"Market revenue > warranty shadow price?"}
    DECIDE -- yes --> DO["Dispatch V2G"]
    DECIDE -- no --> HOLD["Preserve warranty / idle"]
    SOLVE --> COD["Contract co-design: test warranty product variants"]
    COD --> SURPLUS["Joint OEM + fleet surplus per product"]
    SURPLUS --> REC["Recommended warranty product for V2G fleets"]
Why this is the headline: it reframes V2G as a contract-bounded control problem (publishable) and produces a direct MAN sales + product-policy artifact: "here is the warranty we should offer V2G fleets, and why it makes money for everyone." Nobody outside an OEM can credibly write this chapter.

★★ Idea 3 — Manufacturing Firm Ancillary Capacity by Duty-Cycle Co-Scheduling

Novelty: HighFeasibility: Med–High TRATON value: CriticalRole: core / stretch

Gap (verified): FCR needs firm capacity over a product window, but trucks leave the depot. Existing EV-FCR papers treat availability as exogenous and bid under it with chance constraints (Nordic, light-duty). Nobody shapes the logistics to create firm capacity — staggering departures / holding a rotating sub-fleet — turning availability from a constraint into a decision variable, then VPP-aggregating across depots.

4 · ⭐ Recommended fused thesis

"Warranty-Aware, Empirically-Calibrated V2G Dispatch for Heavy-Duty Truck Depots — with a Cross-Market (EU/China) Decision Compiler"

Fuses Idea 2 (headline) + Idea 1 (real-truck anchor) + Idea 6 (interpretable output) + Idea 5 (breadth, job requirement), with Idea 4 as a supporting chapter and Idea 3 / 7 as labelled stretch / future work.

Single research question: How should a heavy-duty truck depot dispatch V2G when the battery warranty is a depletable, shadow-priced contractual resource — and what warranty product makes V2G net-positive for both the OEM and the fleet — validated against measured behaviour of a real truck pack and generalized across EU and Chinese market structures?

5 · EU ↔ China dimension (concrete)

Compile each market into an optimizer config. Axes that actually change dispatch:

  • Grid-fee treatment of stored-then-returned energy — DE just fixed this (single fee from Jan 2026, ⚠ initially tied to own-PV users); FR already favorable; CN differs by province.
  • Market time resolution — EU SDAC is 15-min (since Oct 2025); China spot pilots differ by province.
  • Ancillary access for aggregated EV fleets — who prequalifies, minimum bid size, symmetric vs asymmetric.
  • Capacity / demand charge structure — drives peak-shaving value (German Leistungspreis on peak 15-min).
  • Battery chemistry mix — China skews LFP (V2G-tolerant); EU MAN packs skew NMC (calendar-aging sensitive). The same optimizer behaving differently is a clean result.

6 · Real-truck test plan (days, not fade)

You cannot measure capacity fade in a few days. Measure the inputs your model assumes, and correct them.

7 · Questions to lock the final scope

  1. Warranty access: can you get MAN's actual eTruck battery warranty terms (SoH threshold, year/throughput limits)? Idea 2 needs this; if not, use a representative anonymized version.
  2. Bidirectional test freedom: free to command arbitrary profiles, or only a scripted pilot? Decides how rich the calibration (Idea 1) can be.
  3. Telematics resolution: charger + truck logs at ≤1-min? Needed for taper/delay/thermal capture.
  4. Pack history: age, mileage, cumulative throughput, present SoH for the test truck?
  5. In-house aging model: can you see/modify its equations, or is it a black box? Decides whether calibration is "correct the params" or "wrap a residual model around it."
  6. Deliverable type: publishable method paper, TRATON internal prototype/tool, or future product concept?

8 · Honest bottom line

  • Don't build "a giant Simulink twin" as the contribution — it's an apparatus, and reviewers know it.
  • Do make the headline a new decision function only you can write: warranty as a shadow-priced budget from the OEM seat (Idea 2), made credible by real-truck calibration (Idea 1), generalized by the EU/China compiler (Idea 5), and made tangible by per-truck shadow-price attribution (Idea 6).
  • Stretch / second paper: firm ancillary capacity via duty-cycle co-scheduling (Idea 3).

9 · Review & critique of the "five-family" thesis map (second agent)

A second agent proposed five thesis families plus five flow diagrams. It is a well-structured, lower-risk map — but read honestly, it is also lower-novelty than the warranty angle in §4, because four of the five families are integration of already-published building blocks. Below: each family reproduced with its own diagram, my critique, and a concrete amendment that converts it from "good engineering" into "a defensible research contribution." Two of its diagrams also encode a subtle mistake worth fixing.

The one-line meta-critique: the second map optimizes for defensibility and feasibility; my §4 map optimizes for novelty and OEM-uniqueness. They are not rivals — the second map is the perfect backbone, and my warranty + real-truck-calibration ideas are the novel layer you bolt on top. See §10.

Family 1 — Chemistry-adaptive depot optimizer

Critique: NMC-vs-LFP is well-publishedAmend → life-stage-adaptive

As proposed (their diagram):

flowchart TD
A[Truck arrives at depot] --> B[Read truck data]
B --> C{Battery chemistry?}
C -->|NMC| D[Apply conservative SoC window]
C -->|LFP| E[Apply broader SoC window]
D --> F[Estimate aging cost]
E --> F[Estimate aging cost]
F --> G[Day-ahead MILP schedule]
G --> H[15-min MPC correction]
H --> I{Departure / curtailment / price spike?}
I -->|Yes| H
I -->|No| J[Dispatch charge/discharge plan]

Critique: static "NMC = conservative, LFP = broad" rules are essentially a lookup table, and the NMC/LFP comparison is one of the most published topics in the field. As drawn, the chemistry only changes a fixed SoC window — that is not a research contribution, it is a parameter.

Amendment (the real novelty): make the rules evolve with state-of-health. An NMC pack's voltage curve flattens and its degradation sensitivity drops as it ages toward 80% SoH — it becomes "LFP-like" and tolerates deeper cycling. So the optimizer's constraint set itself should change over the battery's life. Papers model aging's effect on revenue; almost none let aging reshape the optimizer's own rules. That is the defensible function.

flowchart TD
A[Truck arrives] --> B[Read chemistry + SoH + age + cumulative throughput]
B --> C{Life stage?}
C -->|"Fresh NMC (SoH>92%)"| D[Tight SoC window 30-70%, low C-rate]
C -->|"Mid-life NMC (80-92%)"| E[Gradually relax window + C-rate]
C -->|"Aged NMC ~80% / LFP"| F[Broad window, deeper V2G allowed]
D --> G[Aging cost from CALIBRATED surface c SoC,DoD,C,T]
E --> G
F --> G
G --> H[Warranty shadow-price check]
H --> I[Day-ahead MILP -> 15-min MPC]
I --> J[Dispatch + per-truck value attribution]

Critique: this is a textbook MPC loop. It is correct and useful as your platform (it is essentially my §1 layered diagram), but "we built an MPC digital twin" is not novel — heavy-duty truck MPC twins are published (Oulu, NextETRUCK). On its own this risks the "ambitious engineering, not research" verdict.

Amendment: keep this loop as the apparatus, then make the research question the value-of-fidelity ablation (Idea 4): how much does each layer — 15-min vs hourly, nonlinear vs linear aging, stochastic vs deterministic — actually change the dispatch decision and the € outcome? That turns a tool into a finding, and the finding is a firmware spec for the MAN CMS.

Family 3 — V2G + FCR feasibility for heavy trucks (with MCS)

Critique: availability drawn as an INPUTAmend → make it a decision variable

As proposed (their diagram):

flowchart TD

Critique & the subtle mistake: the diagram starts from a given "fleet availability model" and only checks if there is headroom. That is exactly the assumption the published Nordic chance-constrained FCR work already makes for cars — availability is exogenous, you bid under it. For a depot, that throws away your biggest lever.

Amendment (Idea 3): a depot can shape the duty cycle — stagger departures, hold a rotating sub-fleet — to manufacture firm capacity. Availability becomes a decision variable co-optimized with logistics feasibility, with a P99 reliability target. The MCS-vs-CCS branch is good and current (Scania has demonstrated bidirectional V2G over MCS) — keep it as a power-limit parameter.

Critique: structurally sound and industrially relevant, but its novelty (statistical smoothing via law of large numbers) is well understood, and it is data-hungry — you need credible multi-site availability data to prove scalability, which you may not have for a 7–8 month thesis.

Amendment: if real multi-depot data is unavailable, generate it synthetically from one real depot's distributions (bootstrap/Monte-Carlo) and frame the contribution as "how many depots of what availability profile are needed to firm up an X-MW FCR product" — which fuses naturally with Family 3. The revenue-and-risk allocation step is where the shadow-price/dual machinery (Idea 6) earns its keep.

On the second agent's recommended core & titles

Its recommended core — "a chemistry- and market-aware hierarchical optimizer combining day-ahead planning, intra-day MPC, and reserve-service logic under realistic aging constraints" — is a genuinely strong, low-risk systems thesis. My only reservation: its novelty is integration, and integration alone is the most contested form of novelty in front of an examiner. Its five titles are all usable; the strongest is title 3 ("From Depot Charging to Grid Services…") because it promises a result, not just a system.

10 · ⭐ Synthesis verdict — how to use both maps

Decision rule: if you want lower risk and a working system, lead with the backbone and treat warranty shadow-pricing as the "novel contribution" chapter. If you want maximum novelty and publishability, lead with the warranty function and use the hierarchical optimizer as supporting machinery. Either way you ship the same artifacts — only the emphasis changes. The deciding factor is the answer to the §7 questions, above all whether you can get MAN's real warranty terms and free bidirectional test access.
