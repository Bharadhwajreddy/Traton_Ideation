Fresh Lens — broad & novel ideas, and tools you could build

A deliberately wide sweep, written as if starting fresh: AI/ML angles, cross-domain bets, and especially tools and software products — because a working tool is one of the strongest thesis deliverables there is. This is the exploration file; the focused 9-month recommendation still lives in index.html. Honest novelty flags throughout. 20 June 2026.

Why this document

The earlier files converged on one strong, low-risk thesis. This one does the opposite: it opens the aperture all the way, so you can see the full opportunity space and pick deliberately — including the higher-risk, higher-reward, and more "product-like" directions.

Three buckets, by risk:

  • 🛠️ Tools to build — the deliverable is working software/hardware. Lower research risk, very high industrial value, great for a thesis with a demo at the end.
  • 🔬 Novel science bets — a new method or model. Higher novelty, more risk, more publishable.
  • 🌙 Moonshots — broad, ambitious framings. Probably too big alone, but each contains a thesis-sized slice.
Honesty rule For every idea I flag what already exists, so you don't walk into a crowded field thinking it's empty. "Novel" here means novel for heavy-duty trucks, from an OEM seat, with real test access — your unfair advantages.

🛠️ Tools to build (the deliverable is the tool)

T1 · The "5-minute battery fitness test" on the charger strong + timely

A quick diagnostic the charger runs at plug-in that estimates battery health (state of health) in minutes from a short controlled pulse — no full discharge needed — and stamps it into the passport.
flowchart LR
    PLUG["Truck plugs in"] --> PULSE["Short controlled charge/discharge pulse + small partial sweep"]
    PULSE --> FEAT["Extract features: resistance, voltage-vs-charge shape, relaxation"]
    FEAT --> MODEL["Trained model estimates State of Health"]
    MODEL --> OUT["Health number in minutes"]
    OUT --> PP["Write to battery passport"]
    OUT --> CTRL["Feed the charging optimizer"]
In plain words Measuring true battery health normally needs a slow full charge/discharge. This tool estimates it from a quick electrical "poke" plus a model — like a doctor reading your pulse instead of running you on a treadmill for an hour.
Why it's valuable & timely There is currently no agreed standard for measuring EV-pack health (flagged in a 2025 npj Clean Energy paper), yet the battery passport requires a health value from 2027. A fast, on-charger estimator is exactly the missing piece — and a clean product for MAN. Your real truck/bench is perfect for building and validating it.
What exists Rapid-SoH estimation from pulses/impedance exists for cells and cars (lots of ML papers). For heavy-duty truck packs, in-depot, tied to the passport — much thinner. That truck-pack + passport framing is your novelty.

T2 · "V2G Profitability Sandbox" — a configurator anyone can use

A web tool: a fleet operator types in their depot (trucks, missions, chemistry, country) and instantly sees revenue, battery wear and total cost of ownership for different strategies.
flowchart LR
    IN["Inputs: #trucks, missions, chemistry, country, charger type"] --> ENGINE["Engine: wear map + optimizer + market rules"]
    ENGINE --> OUT1["Revenue by service"]
    ENGINE --> OUT2["Battery wear / lifetime"]
    ENGINE --> OUT3["Total cost of ownership"]
    OUT1 --> UI["Interactive dashboard + downloadable report"]
    OUT2 --> UI
    OUT3 --> UI
In plain words Think "mortgage calculator," but for truck V2G. It turns all the modelling into a few sliders and a clear chart, so a non-expert can explore "what if."
Why it's a great thesis The deliverable is a working tool with a demo — examiners and managers both love that. It also forces you to make the science usable, and it naturally showcases the wear map and the cross-country results.
Watch out Make the method behind it the contribution (e.g. the wear map or the optimizer), so it's a thesis and not "just a calculator." The tool is the wrapper.

T3 · "Charging-brain-as-a-service" — a learned controller API

Package the optimizer as a tiny service any charger can call: send the battery state + passport, get back the best charge/V2G action in milliseconds — fast enough to run on real hardware.
flowchart LR
    TRAIN["Offline: train a fast policy to imitate the slow optimizer (+ wear map)"] --> POLICY["Compact learned policy"]
    CHARGER["Charger / depot controller"] --> API["API call: state + passport + prices"]
    API --> POLICY
    POLICY --> ACT["Action: power, charge window, V2G yes/no (in ms)"]
    ACT --> CHARGER
In plain words The full optimizer is powerful but slow. You train a small, fast "imitator" that gives nearly the same answers instantly — small enough to live inside the charger's software. The thesis question: how close can the fast version get to the slow optimal one?
Why it matters This is the bridge from "nice simulation" to "runs in the real MAN charging management system." It directly answers a product question.
What exists "Learning to optimize" / imitation of MILP/MPC exists in power systems. Tied to a measured truck wear map + passport, for HD depots, it's fresh.

T4 · A hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) test rig uses your real truck

Connect the real truck/charger to a simulated grid, market and depot — so the physical hardware reacts to fake price spikes and grid events you design, safely and repeatably.
flowchart LR
    SIM["Simulated grid + market + other trucks"] --> CMD["Send commands / price signals"]
    CMD --> REAL["REAL truck + charger"]
    REAL --> MEAS["Measure real response: power, delay, efficiency, temperature"]
    MEAS --> SIM
    MEAS --> VALID["Validate the controller against reality"]
In plain words Instead of waiting for a real price spike, you invent one in software and watch how the real truck actually responds. The hardware is real; the world around it is simulated. This is how you test a controller before trusting it with money.
Why it's strong for you It turns your "a few days with a real truck" into a reusable platform, and it makes every later result credible because the controller was tested on real hardware, not just in a model.

T5 · An open benchmark + dataset for HD-truck depot V2G

Release a standard test problem (and data) so different methods can be compared fairly — a "common yardstick" the field currently lacks for heavy trucks.
In plain words Today everyone tests their charging optimizer on their own private setup, so results can't be compared. A shared benchmark fixes that — and whoever creates it gets cited a lot.
Why it matters High academic impact for relatively contained effort; positions MAN/TRATON as the reference point. Pairs well with any of the method ideas.
Watch out Data-sharing approval can be slow — check what MAN will let you release (anonymised/synthetic may be the answer).

🔬 Novel science bets (a new method or model)

S1 · Hybrid physics + AI aging twin

Keep the trusted physics aging model, and add a small AI layer that learns the leftover error from real data — best of both: physically sensible and accurate.
flowchart LR
    PHYS["Physics aging model"] --> SUM
    DATA["Real truck/bench data"] --> ML["AI learns the residual error"]
    ML --> SUM["Combined estimate"]
    SUM --> OUT["Accurate, physically-consistent wear prediction"]
Why Pure physics is interpretable but imperfect; pure AI is accurate but a black box and needs lots of data. The hybrid needs less data and stays trustworthy — ideal when you only have one truck.
Exists Physics-informed / residual ML for batteries is an active area for cells. For a real HD truck pack, calibrated from your tests, it's fresh and very defensible.

S2 · Federated learning across the fleet (privacy-preserving) genuinely novel here

Learn one shared battery-health/wear model from many MAN trucks worldwide — without moving their raw data — by sharing only model updates.
flowchart TB
    D1["Truck/depot A learns locally"] --> AGG["Aggregate only the model updates (no raw data)"]
    D2["Truck/depot B learns locally"] --> AGG
    D3["Truck/depot C learns locally"] --> AGG
    AGG --> GLOBAL["Improved global wear / health model"]
    GLOBAL --> D1
    GLOBAL --> D2
    GLOBAL --> D3
In plain words Every MAN truck is a tiny experiment in battery aging. Federated learning lets them all teach one shared model without any depot exposing its private data — solving the data-ownership problem that usually blocks fleet-scale learning.
Why it's exciting for an OEM MAN has thousands of trucks but the data is fragmented and sensitive. This is the realistic way to turn that fleet into one giant learning system — a genuine strategic capability, not just a thesis.
Exists Federated learning for battery SoH is just emerging (a few 2023–2025 papers). For heavy-duty fleets it is wide open — a strong, modern, publishable bet.

S3 · Reinforcement-learning, degradation-aware dispatch

Train an agent by trial-and-error (in simulation) to balance earning money now against wearing the battery — then compare it head-to-head with the classic optimizer.
In plain words Instead of solving an equation each time, an RL agent learns a habit for good decisions. Useful when the future is uncertain and you must react fast.
Watch out RL for EV charging is crowded and often beaten by simpler MPC. Only do this if your twist is real — e.g. learning directly against the measured wear map, or safety-guaranteed RL that never violates the warranty budget.

S4 · Carbon-aware V2G — trucks as mobile decarbonisation fresh framing

Optimise not just euros but grams of CO₂ avoided: charge when the grid is clean, discharge when it's dirty — and report the verified emissions saved.
flowchart LR
    SIG["Live grid CO2 intensity + price"] --> OPT["Multi-objective optimizer: euros AND carbon"]
    OPT --> PLAN["Charge clean hours, discharge dirty hours"]
    PLAN --> REP["Verified avoided-emissions report"]
    REP --> ESG["Feeds customer ESG / CSRD reporting"]
In plain words A parked truck battery can soak up surplus wind/solar and give it back when the grid would otherwise burn gas. This idea measures and maximises that climate benefit — and produces a number a customer can put in their sustainability report.
Why it matters Money isn't the only currency anymore — EU sustainability reporting (CSRD) makes verified CO₂ savings valuable to fleets. A "V2G carbon" angle is timely and relatively under-explored for trucks.
Exists Carbon-aware charging exists for data centres and some EVs; tying it to HD-truck V2G + degradation cost + verified reporting is the fresh combination.

S5 · Incentive & contract design — getting humans to cooperate

Design the pricing/incentives so drivers and depots naturally do the grid-friendly thing (arrive with the right charge, allow V2G) — a mechanism-design problem, not a physics one.
In plain words The best schedule is useless if a driver plugs in late or refuses V2G. This idea asks: what reward structure makes the selfish choice also the optimal one? It's economics/game theory applied to a depot.
Why it's different Almost all V2G work assumes perfect compliance. The human-incentive layer is real, under-studied, and exactly where flexibility is won or lost in practice.

S6 · Flexibility forecasting as a product

Predict, days ahead, how much grid flexibility a depot will have (from routes, telematics, weather) — and sell that forecast to aggregators/markets.
In plain words Markets pay for reliable promises. If you can forecast "next Tuesday this depot can offer 2 MW from 22:00–02:00," that forecast itself is valuable. The thesis: how accurately can fleet flexibility be predicted, and what is a good forecast worth?
Why Reframes the problem from control to prediction — a cleaner, more self-contained thesis, and a product an aggregator would buy.

🌙 Moonshots (think very broadly — each hides a thesis-sized slice)

W1 · Roaming / spatial V2G — the truck earns wherever it goes

The passport travels with the battery, so any charger on the route can make a safe, history-aware V2G decision — turning a moving truck into a roaming grid asset across regions and price zones.
In plain words Today V2G is a home-depot idea. If the battery's history is portable (passport), a truck could sell energy in whichever region has the best prices that day — "spatial arbitrage" on wheels.
Thesis-sized slice "How much extra value comes from being able to do V2G away from the home depot?" — a clean, bounded question inside the big vision.

W2 · Battery-swap + V2G + second-life, as one economic system

If packs can be swapped, the depot's batteries become a shared pool — some driving, some doing V2G, some resting or heading to second life. Optimise the whole pool.
Thesis-sized slice "When does decoupling the battery from the truck (swap) beat fixed packs for combined driving + V2G value?" Big and forward-looking; pick one sub-question.

W3 · Verifiable V2G credentials — a trust layer for settlement

Tamper-proof, signed records of what each truck actually delivered to the grid and how much life it cost — so payments, warranty claims and CO₂ credits can be trusted automatically.
Reality More of an IT/standards contribution than control. A neat niche if you like data integrity, but probably a side-feature, not the core.

W4 · Thermal co-optimisation — schedule heat, charge and V2G together

Battery pre-heating/cooling, cabin comfort, charging and V2G all share energy and affect aging. Optimise them jointly instead of separately.
Why it's interesting Temperature is one of the biggest aging drivers, and trucks in cold climates spend real energy on it. Coupling thermal management with the V2G plan is a genuine, cross-domain optimisation few have done for HD trucks.
